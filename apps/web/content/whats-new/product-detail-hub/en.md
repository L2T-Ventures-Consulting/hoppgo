Until now, clicking a product opened its edit form. That was all. Nowhere told you what a product earns, whether it goes out, or what happened to that one unit you have been looking for since Tuesday.

## Four figures at the top

- **Revenue** generated by this product, with how it moved over the last 30 days.
- **Reservations**, broken down into upcoming and already completed.
- **Utilisation rate** over 30 days: the share of the time the product was actually out.
- **Stock** — the quantity available, or the number of active units out of the total if you track them one by one.

The utilisation rate is the one that surprises people: it tells you which gear is sleeping at the back of the depot.

## Inventory, on the spot

The Inventory section replaces the page of the same name. In simple quantity mode it shows stock and what remains available. With per-unit tracking it lists your units with their identifier, their variants and their state — and you declare downtime or retire a unit without changing page.

## The product's schedule

This product's reservations read as a list or as a calendar. The calendar shows one row per unit and what stays available on each date, and you can **drag across a free slot** to start a reservation with its dates set and this product already in it.

## Every unit's log

If you track your units one by one, a log follows their life: created, assigned to a reservation, put in downtime, repaired, retired, reinstated. It is what you look at when a customer returns a damaged bike and you want to know who had it before.

## And the rest within reach

In the right-hand column, the key facts: deposit, VAT — yours or the one inherited from the store — creation date and last change. Below, the description, seasonal pricing, accessories and the gallery. One button opens the page as your customers see it, another the edit form.
